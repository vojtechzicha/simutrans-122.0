Platforms: what leaves from where

The game writes the signs you now write by hand. Every station track and every bus or tram stand gets a card on the map with its number, the lines that board there and the direction each one leaves in. The cards follow the schedules, so they are never out of date. The same numbers show on the departure boards, in the convoy window and on optional displays and signs.

No screenshots arrived with your message, so I loaded your save of 8 November into the test harness and looked at Hranice. Every picture below is your Hranice na Moravě at 11:20 on 10 May 1931, with the new parts drawn in the game's own font and colours. As a check, the renderer redrew your real 13-row train board with no pixel different (0 of 221,760).

Platform cards, written by the game

The same view with your markers removed and the cards drawn from the schedules in the save. The numbers are the ones on your markers (track 7 from "N3/7", stands 1 to 15).

  1. A platform is a station track, or a stand: the tiles where one bus or tram stops, in one travel direction.
  2. Lines are the line numbers the departure board shows, for every line with at least one vehicle that takes passengers on board there.
  3. Groups by next stop. Lines that go on to the same next stop share a group. The next stop is the next place in the schedule where passengers can get off; waypoints, Hold stops and depots are skipped.
  4. The arrow points the way the vehicle leaves, on the screen. It replaces "směr". Trains for Olomouc leave Hranice to the upper left, trains for Ostrava to the lower right.
  5. The next stop is named only when it says something: the lines of a platform split up (track 7), or a line also boards at another platform of the stop for another next stop (Ex2 on tracks 2 and 3). Stand 9 has one line going one way and shows just the chip.
  6. A line that leaves two ways appears in both groups (930 on stand 11).
  7. Arrivals only. A platform where a line only lets people off shows "arr." and the line, like your "příjezd".
  8. Tracks show both the next stop and the destination. A track's card is a small table: one row per next stop on the left, and on the right every line with where it ends. Trains need no switching: a track has few lines, and your own track markers already name destinations. Track 7 takes four rows where your marker takes one line of about 970 pixels.
  9. Stands show the next stop, because a kerb can carry twenty lines. A stop's stands can be switched to name destinations too (next section).

Stands at some stops: next stop and destination

Tracks always show both. Stands show the next stop until you switch the stop; then each line also names where it ends, which is what your bus-station markers do ("1: 345 Olomouc, 521 Lipník n.B."). The switch is kept per stop and per kind, so at Hranice it changes the bus stands and leaves the tracks alone. If a big station ever gets crowded, the same switch on a track card makes that stop's tracks compact (next stop only).

From the stop window

The Platforms tab has a button that reads Stands: next stop. Press it and it reads Stands: next stop + destination. A stop with tracks has the same button for them. The choice is kept with the stop in the save.

Straight on the map

Shift+click a stand's card switches that stop's stands between the two forms, without opening a window. A plain click still opens the platform window.

Set for you at the start

The marker import switches a stop to the second form when your markers there name a destination after a line number, and lists those stops in its report. Your bus stations start the way you already label them.

City stops: a layout the markers could not have

A marker is one line of text centred above a tile. A card is drawn by the game, so it can do more. Line numbers are chips that wrap six to a row, so twenty lines make a small block. Each kerb has its own card, on its own side of the road, tied to the stand by a short leader.

The card in all its forms

  1. Row 1: track 7 by next stop, the default.
  2. Row 2: the same track as tracks show by default: a row per next stop, every line with where it ends. Row 1 is its compact form.
  3. Row 3: direction only where it tells something; a stand without a number; arrivals only; arrivals next to departures.
  4. Row 4: many lines wrap six to a row. After three rows the rest becomes "+4". A kerb shared by trams (red) and buses (blue) keeps them in separate groups.
  5. Row 5, left: zoomed out one step, cards shrink to chips and arrows. Further out they hide.
  6. Row 5, right: pointing at a card opens it: every line with the place it ends.

When cards show

A view switch with three positions, on a key and in the display settings: off, chosen stops (the default, as you confirmed), all stops. A stop is chosen with one button in its window and stays chosen in the save. Whatever the switch says, the stop under the mouse and a stop with its window open show their cards.

Where cards sit

A track's card starts at the end of the platform that is further left on screen. A stand's card sits beside its kerb, to the right of the travel direction, so the two directions of a street stop never share a side. A card that would cover the stop's name or another card moves down a row.

What a click does

Clicking a card opens the platform window; Shift+click switches the stop's form. Clicking a line chip in the window opens that line. Cards do not block building: with a build tool selected, clicks pass through them.

How it works on the road

A road tile serves both directions, so a tile alone cannot be a platform. The stand is the unit.

The numbers on the boards

The train board gets a Track column, the bus and tram board a Stand column. Choose signals and P signals pick the real track on the way in, so the board says how sure it is.

7 Planned: the schedule's track; nothing is reserved yet. 7 Confirmed: standing there, or the way in is reserved or claimed. 3 Changed: sent elsewhere. Blinks; the planned number is crossed out below.

The schedule editor shows the same number after each stop in the entry list ("Hranice na Moravě, track 7"), and the tooltip names the track or stand while you pick a tile. No message is posted when a track changes.

The Platforms tab

One list of the stop's tracks and stands, in the stop window. It is where you number them and where you see the whole stop at once.

  • No. is one short text, up to 7 characters. Type it here, or use Number tracks and click a platform in the world.
  • Map labels: this stop chooses the stop for the "chosen stops" view.
  • Show: next stop switches this stop to next stop + destination and back.
  • Numbers from markers reads the numbers off your markers at this stop (see below).
  • New tracks are numbered across the station as they appear and keep their number afterwards. Stands stay without one.
  • A platform where no line boards is listed, so you can see unused tracks.

Displays and signs you can build

Cards are information. These are decoration for the stations you care about: pak objects placed with the sign tool, with text drawn by the game. A display shows the next departure only, one row per track, and alternates the destination with "VIA".

  • Two road sign flags (bits 13 and 14 are free): PLATFORM_DISPLAY and TRACK_SIGN, for rail, tram and road. They never stop a vehicle and no search sees them.
  • A tile holds one sign, as now. On a road tile the sign serves one kerb or both, cycled by clicking again like a signal. Ctrl+click puts a sign on every stand of the stop.
  • The text uses a 3×5 LED font with Czech accents and steps down one pixel for every two along, the slope of the tile edge. It is drawn at the normal zoom and closer.

Moving over from your markers

Two batch tools, built like -strip-parking: a dry run first, a report for every marker, a backup, and nothing touched that the tool is not sure about.

1. Take the numbers

steam-fork.sh platform-numbers NAME reads markers within one tile of a platform that start with N3/7:, K2:, 14 –, J – or 3: and gives the platform that number (from "N3/7" the track, 7). A marker that covers two stands ("5: … | 6: …") is reported and left for you. On your save 1,691 markers qualify. The same run switches stops whose markers name destinations to the second form.

2. Remove the line lists

steam-fork.sh strip-line-markers NAME removes a marker at a stop only when every line number in it is on that stop's cards (numbers in <…> are ignored). Markers with anything else stay: "zaměstnanecké linky", "nevyužito", "ostatní", and all your road signs.

3. Check the rest

The report lists what was kept and why, sorted by town, so the leftovers are a short list to walk through. You can also keep any marker for good by naming its stop in a keep file.

Under the hood

Data

Each stop keeps a list of platforms: the tiles, the travel direction for stands, the number and whether it was typed. The lines of a platform are worked out, not saved, whenever a line starts or stops calling or a schedule changes; this is the pass that already rebuilds the stop's connections. Nothing is computed per frame.

Save and export

Savegame 122.18: numbers, the chosen-stop switch, the form of each stop and each line's stand direction. Older saves load with tracks numbered across. -saveversion 0.122.0 leaves it all out. The JSON export gains platforms per stop and platform per convoy and schedule entry, and -rename takes PLATFORM rows.

Drawing

Cards are drawn in the label pass, for visible stops only, from a layout kept per stop. The layout is a flow of group cells: chips six to a row, then arrow and name; cells wrap at 420 pixels for tracks and 190 for stands. Chosen stops are few, and the all-stops view lays out only what is on screen.

Tests

Headless scenarios with new checks: the platforms of a layout and their lines (expect_platform), stand direction on a through tile, numbers kept through extending, cutting and joining a stop, save and load, a choose signal turning a track from planned to changed.

In what order

Each step is usable on its own.

  1. Platforms and cards. Tracks and stands, stand direction, lines by next stop, the cards with all three views, the Platforms tab and the platform window. This alone replaces the hand-written line lists.
  2. Numbers. Typing and the numbering tool, the marker import and strip tools, save format 122.18, the export.
  3. Boards. Track and Stand columns with the three states, the convoy window, the schedule editor.
  4. Displays and signs. The two sign flags, makeobj keys, the LED text, pakset art.

Decided

  • One number per platform.
  • Tracks show next stop and destination together, with no switching. Stands show the next stop; a stop's stands can be switched to next stop + destination in its Platforms tab, by Shift+click on a card, or by the marker import.
  • The default view is chosen stops plus the stop under the mouse.
  • Displays say "VIA" and show the next departure only. No message on a track change.
